Grand Prix privacy policy

Grand Prix is a text editor for Google Drive files. It is serverless and stores no data except settings, which are kept in local storage and a single Google Drive file controlled by Google. It's fairly farcical I have to write a privacy policy, so if you're actually reading this: I see nothing you don't sent deliberately, and mostly not that either. Google does, and I can't control what Google does with your files and settings, which are stored in Google Drive. There is nothing to hide and nothing to take or use. If you care about the meaningless legalese... it's all technically true. I guess. So sure, keep reading.

The app runs entirely in your browser. There is no server of mine behind it, and I never receive, collect, or store your data. The static files are served by a hosting provider that keeps ordinary web server request logs under its own policies. The page also loads Google's sign-in and API client scripts directly from Google, which are covered by Google's privacy policy.

Signing in to Google is optional; the editor works without it. If you sign in, the app can read and write only the Drive files you open or create with it, and one hidden settings file in the private application data folder Google provides for this app. It cannot see your other Drive files.

Your browser's local storage holds the Google sign-in token, its expiry, and your editor preferences, so that you do not have to sign in on every page load. This data never leaves your machine except in requests to Google's APIs.

I cannot share, sell, or use your data for any purpose beyond the editor itself. The app's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To remove what the app has stored, clear this site's data in your browser, delete the app's hidden data from Drive's app settings, and revoke the app's access at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Questions can be sent to grand-prix@cupchinski.com.

This app has never been the subject of a warrant that would require it to give away information it collected. And probably never will be. It is likely that the app will be taken down if that ever happens, and only this page (with this paragraph removed) will remain up. It will have a date in that case. No change to this file is anticipated in any other case for any reason.
